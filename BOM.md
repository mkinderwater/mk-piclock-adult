# mk-clock-adult 2.1A BPI-M2 Zero R1 Bill of Materials

This is the reference purchase list for the BPI-M2 Zero adult clock. It records the exact parts selected for the build, what each part is used for, and the reason for the selection.

## Cost basis

Costs are shown in **Canadian dollars (CAD)** and are intended as purchase-reference costs, not an exact per-clock material cost. Several items are sold in multi-packs and are shared across multiple clocks.

Price status was checked **2026-08-08**. Amazon sometimes hides a price until an item is placed in the cart, and several of these listings currently have no featured offer. Those entries are marked **Not publicly shown** rather than assigning an unsupported price. Taxes and shipping are not included.

The 52Pi store currently shows the BPI-M2 Zero at `$22.94`. The CAD reference below assumes that store price is USD and converts it at the Bank of Canada 2026-08-07 USD/CAD daily rate of `1.3943`, giving approximately **C$31.99**.

## Component pairing rationale

The audio chain is intentionally simple:

```text
BPI-M2 Zero I2S -> MAX98357A -> 4 ohm / 3 W speaker
```

Audio remains digital until the Class-D amplifier. This avoids a separate USB or analog sound device.

The display and environmental sensor use separate buses:

```text
SSD1322 OLED -> SPI0
AHT10        -> I2C0
```

The OLED can therefore update quickly without interfering with the low-rate environmental measurements.

The external antenna is used because the clock is a continuously connected appliance. NTP, ECCC Weather retrieval and the web interface all benefit from additional Wi-Fi signal margin once the BPI is enclosed.

## Other required build items

The supplied purchase list does not include every external requirement. The completed clock also needs:

- **Regulated 5 V power source**. Board power is +5 V to CON2 physical pin 4 and GND to physical pin 6. Do not power the board from two sources at once.
- **microSD card** containing the validated Armbian image and clock software. Use a card proven to boot reliably on the BPI-M2 Zero. The previously tested Gigastone MLC card was not reliable on this platform, while a basic compatible microSD card boots correctly.
